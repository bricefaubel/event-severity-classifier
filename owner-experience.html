The owner doesn't have a queue. They have one question.

A dealer works a list. An owner has a single system and, almost always, nothing wrong with it — so the surface has to be built for the empty state first. And the question they're actually asking isn't how severe is this, it's who has the ball: do I need to do something, is my dealer already on it, or is this just being watched? Severity still sets the channel and the urgency. It never reaches the owner as a word.

What actually arrives

The month, not the moment

Every alert design reviews well one alert at a time. The failure happens in aggregate: an owner who gets pinged weekly turns notifications off, permanently, and the channel you needed for a combustion event is gone. This is the same thirty days of equipment behaviour, counted from both sides.

Rules for the owner side

These are the ones that don't exist on the dealer side at all. The dealer's queue is a work tool they opted into; the owner's phone is not.

Translation

The tier never crosses over

No owner sees the words Critical, Medium or Low, and no owner sees a fault code. They get the stake — the house, the air, the product in the cooler, the bill — plus who is handling it. Severity decides the channel and the timing behind the glass.

Sequencing

The dealer is told first, except for safety

Notify an owner before their dealer has triaged and you generate a phone call the dealer can't answer yet. Everything non-safety waits for acknowledgment or a short timer. Safety fires to both at once — that's the only simultaneous case.

Quiet hours

Don't wake someone for something they can't fix at 2am

A Critical the owner can't act on overnight is held until morning; the dealer still receives it immediately, so nothing is lost. Safety ignores quiet hours entirely — that's the whole reason the exception exists.

Budget

Roughly one push a month, or the channel dies

Owner tolerance is an order of magnitude below a dealer's. Every Medium promoted to push spends against the one notification that will matter later. When in doubt, put it in the app and let them find it.

Withholding

Unconfirmed signals don't reach the owner at all

An inferred pattern is a dealer's lead, not an owner's news. It sits on the watch list until a technician confirms it. Telling an owner their system "might" have a problem transfers anxiety without transferring any ability to act.

Language

No dollar figures, and no diagnosis

The product describes the condition and names who is responding. It does not estimate repair cost, project savings, or state a cause — those are the dealer's to make and the dealer's to stand behind.

Business owner

Delegation is a first-class case

A business owner may not be the person who acts. The alert names the site, routes to the facilities contact, and escalates on a timer if nobody acknowledges — a chain a homeowner never needs and a homeowner surface can't express.

Empty state

The default screen is "nothing wrong," and it has to earn its keep

An owner opens the app to a healthy system almost every time. If that screen only says "OK," there's no reason to open it twice. It's the one surface where quiet compensation — the Low tier — has real value to show.
