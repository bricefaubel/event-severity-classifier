Three tiers, because a dealer has three responses.

Severity here is remaining margin, not imagined consequence — how much reserve the system has left before it stops holding the line. Margin is observable, which means the product can assign the tier instead of asking a person to. Each tier maps to one thing a dealer actually does: go today, put it on the schedule, or catch it on a visit already booked.

Try one
01Which domain does this belong to?

Domain is a routing axis, not a severity input. It decides who receives the event, what the action is, and which revenue motion it feeds — but on its own it doesn't make anything urgent.

02Does the same condition touch other domains?

Most real conditions light up several at once. One condition produces one row at its highest tier, with the others carried as named impacts — otherwise the queue triple-counts and the badge lies to a service manager staffing a day.

03How much margin is left?

This is the spine. Equipment rarely fails discretely — it compensates, then compensates harder, then can't. Each rung is something a connected system can observe directly.

04How often?

Repetition promotes the rung, not the tier — spending reserve once is an observation, spending it nightly is a condition. This is why the object being classified is a condition over time, with a first-seen date and a count, not a stream of events.

05When does it stop holding?

This is what replaced the fourth tier. "On the brink" inside the response window is Critical; "on the brink" in six weeks is planned work. Time splits the rung — confidence no longer does.

06Who is in the building, and when?

Identical margin, different tier. A school at 7am Monday and the same school at 2am Saturday are not the same event, and a product that can't tell them apart will either over-alert or miss the one that mattered.

07How do we know?

Demoted from a tier to a qualifier. Evidence quality is a statement about proof, not about urgency — it changes the verb, the channel, and what the product is allowed to claim. It does not move the tier.

08Is there something the recipient can do?

The gate between an alert and a watch item. An alert nobody can close teaches people to stop reading the queue.

03What is the state?

State has no margin dimension and no lifecycle — it can't accrue, recur, or worsen on its own, so it isn't severity. It's a field on the record. The exception is named below.

How this was decided

Three tiers, defined by response

Each tier is named for what it commits someone to. If nobody is on the hook for the window, the tier is a lie and the one below it is the honest answer. Info sits outside the scale entirely — it isn't a quieter severity, it's a different kind of fact.

Action by domain and tier

The failure mode of the previous framework was an action column that didn't vary — three of four tiers dispatched a technician, and the middle tier said "update your set-point schedules" even in rows where set points aren't the lever. If two tiers produce the same action, only the adjective changed. Every cell here is a different response.

Rules the classifier enforces

The rungs get you a defensible default. These are the cases where the default is wrong, and each one exists because it has already gone badly somewhere.

Floor

Safety never drops below Critical once margin is gone

If the domain is safety and the function has stopped or is at its limit, no modifier and no confidence level can bring the tier down. Uncertainty changes the wording, not the response.

Qualifier, not tier

Confidence changes the sentence, not the urgency

A predicted failure is as urgent as a confirmed one if it lands inside the same window. What confidence controls: the verb, whether the product may state a commitment, and whether a Critical is allowed to wake someone by SMS.

Promotion

Repetition moves the rung, not the tier

Spending reserve once is an observation. Spending it nightly is a condition. The object therefore needs a first-seen date and a count, and the pipeline must roll duplicates up rather than emit a row each time.

Context

Occupancy is part of severity

Sensitive occupancy promotes safety and comfort by one tier. An empty building demotes comfort by one — never safety, never system health. Unknown occupancy is treated as occupied, never as empty.

Sorting

Low and Info are separated by whether it can change

If it accrues, recurs, or worsens on its own, it is Low — a watch item with a date and a count. If it is a fact about right now with no lifecycle, it is Info. "Needs no action today" is not the test; both pass it.

Roll-up

One condition, one row, highest tier

A failing compressor is system health, comfort and energy at once. It appears once at its highest tier, with the other domains named as impacts, so the badge count stays a number a service manager can staff against.

Language

Machine severity doesn't quote money

Automated diagnosis paired with a dollar figure is a commercial representation the product may have to defend. State the condition and the domain impact; let the dealer price it.

Budget

Critical is capped, not extended

If more than a small share of live conditions sit at Critical, the tier has stopped meaning anything. The fix is demotion and better horizon data — never a fourth tier above it.

What changed from the four-tier model

High disappeared as a rung and came back as two better-behaved inputs. "On the brink" is now split by when rather than by how sure, which removes the old bug where every predictive signal automatically ranked below every confirmed one — something that was never true. And the gray column stopped being a graveyard: quiet compensation is the earliest planned-repair signal a dealer gets, so it sits on a watch list that promotes itself, rather than expiring next to connectivity status.

Does it survive a real queue?

A classifier that produces clean rulings one at a time can still fail the only test that matters — whether a service manager can find the two conditions that change their day among sixteen that don't. Not in the queue — state, not severity

These are facts about the product, not about the equipment. They live on the record and never take a row, a color, or a place in the badge count. The moment one of them hides a monitoring commitment the dealer sold, it becomes a System health condition and enters the queue on its own terms.

What each control is testing

These aren't demo toggles. Each one corresponds to a way this queue will actually be encountered — on a phone in a truck, at a glance between calls, sorted by someone who prefers chronology, on a bad month.

No color

Roughly one in twelve technicians can't use red-versus-amber

Strip saturation and the hierarchy has to survive on fill, weight, border style and position. Critical is the only filled row; Low is the only dashed one. If you can still sort the list without hue, the tiers are carried by structure rather than decoration.

Squint

The glance test, at arm's length in a truck

Blur removes everything but mass and position. Two dark blocks should remain legible at the top. If Medium and Critical smear into the same shape, the treatment gap between "go today" and "book it" is too small.

By recency / by site

Users re-sort, and grouping is not a safety net

Grouping by response makes priority trivially findable — which is cheating. Break the grouping and the row treatment has to carry it alone. This is the honest test, and the one most severity systems fail.

Flood

The Critical budget, made visible

Adding eighteen more Low conditions is what a bad month or a loose threshold looks like. Critical count stays at two. If the top of the queue still reads clearly, the tier is holding its meaning; if it drowns, the answer is demotion, not a new tier above it.

Dense

Scanning versus reading

Drop the condition line and the row is reduced to subject, tier and tags. A service manager assigning a day works in this mode. Anything the model needs to communicate has to survive the loss of the sentence.

Grid

The layout that takes the alignment away

Rows give you a shared left edge, so the eye compares tier labels down a single column. A grid removes that entirely — each card has to declare its own priority. Critical answers by filling and spanning two columns, which makes mass the carrier rather than hue or position, and mass is the one channel that survives both greyscale and blur.

Look for

The two rows carrying a qualifier

One Medium is marked Predicted, one Low is marked Unconfirmed. Their tiers come from margin and horizon; the flag only tells you how much to trust the sentence. Confidence changed the label, not the position — that's the v2 change made visible.
